# Lore Bible — Untitled Dungeon Crawler
### Built with Ben, Session 4 (in progress)

## Core Premise (locked in)
**Story structure (locked in): Chapters = Levels.** Each dungeon level is a chapter — one fallen rival-party member (broken torch), a puzzle, something new, and a story reveal per level/chapter, per the structure decided earlier.

**The Rival Party.** Another group of adventurers went down into the dungeon before your party — and never came back out. Your party is following their trail: finding their leftover gear, their notes, and their warnings scattered through the dungeon as you go.

**The mission (locked in):** The king secretly sent the rival party into the dungeon on a covert quest — to stop a dark wizard from tearing open a portal that would let demonic forces invade this world. It was kept secret. *(Still to decide: why — panic prevention, political embarrassment, something else?)*

**The rival party's leader (locked in):** was a close friend of the protagonist.

**Protagonist's motivation (locked in):** **Hope.** The protagonist refuses to believe their friend is dead and goes into the dungeon searching for them — not for treasure, not just duty. This means every broken torch/fallen party member found along the way lands harder: each one is a step closer to an outcome the protagonist doesn't want to face, right up until the leader's own torch is found.

**The dread mechanic (locked in):** the rival party was genuinely strong/capable — stronger than your own party. Finding their remains level by level recalibrates the fear: if people this powerful didn't survive, what chance does your smaller party have? This also justifies increasing difficulty as you descend.

**Structure (locked in, ties directly to the title):** one fallen rival-party member is found per level/chapter, each with a **broken torch** — a direct callback to the game's title, "The Last Torchlight." Finding them delivers a puzzle, something new (monster/weapon/spell), and a story reveal — confirming the "chapters" story-structure option from Session 3's discussion.

This gives us a built-in, natural way to deliver lore and hints: a lot of what the player finds can be framed as **things the rival party left behind** — a torn journal page, a dropped weapon with a name scratched into it, a warning scrawled on a wall in a hurry.

**Secrecy (locked in):** the king kept the mission secret out of **fear of panic** — word of demons and a portal to another world would cause chaos across the kingdom. This means the rival party went in quietly, with no public support or backup, which reinforces the dread mechanic: powerful people failed, and almost no one even knew they'd tried.

**The dark wizard/villain (locked in):** not originally from this world — their own homeworld already fell to the same demonic forces years ago, and they lost everything: home, family, everyone. Their desperation isn't a desire to invade — it's a twisted hope that reopening the portal could save whoever or whatever might still be alive on the other side. The tragedy: the portal doesn't discriminate. Reopening it risks letting the same demonic forces that destroyed their world through into this one, dooming this world exactly as theirs was doomed. They may be in denial about that risk, or simply willing to gamble everything on it.

**Thematic parallel (worth keeping in mind):** the protagonist is searching for someone they lost (hope), and the villain is also driven by loss — just resolving it in a far more dangerous way. Neither is straightforwardly "good" or "evil" on the surface; both are trying to hold onto someone/something.

**The wizard's identity (locked in): Ashwren Vale, the Last Scholar.** Once a royal archivist in their homeworld, specializing in dimensional theory purely as academic curiosity — never intended to actually open anything. When the demonic incursion began, Ashwren spent the invasion trying to *seal* doorways shut, not open them, and failed. They survived by hiding in a sealed archive while their world fell around them, and have carried survivor's guilt ever since. Now, desperate, they believe reopening a connection is the only way to find out if anyone else made it out too — a total reversal of the person they used to be.

## Bestiary — Progression Theme (locked in)
Four tiers, tied directly to Ashwren's failing seal — the corruption gets worse the deeper you go:

**Tier 0 — Not Corrupted** (nothing magical wrong yet — mundane dungeon threats)
- **Dungeon Rats** — ordinary vermin. Weak to Strength.
- **Rusted Sentinel** — old mechanical/wind-up guardian construct, mundane engineering, not magic. Weak to Finesse (exploit joints/gaps).
- **Cave Bats** — erratic, hard to hit physically; a calming/disruption spell settles their frenzy. Weak to Intelligence.
- **Skeleton Warrior** — classic reanimated dungeon guardian, sword and shield, animated by old residual dungeon magic unrelated to Ashwren/the portal. Weak to Strength (shatter the bones).

**Tier 1 — Semi-Corrupted** (leaking dark magic starting to twist things)
- **Blightrat Swarm** — rats warped and made aggressive. Weak to Strength.
- **Warden of Ash** — a dungeon guard's armor, animated and corroded by corruption. Weak to Finesse.
- **Murkwisp** — a twisted, flickering light that lures explorers off safe paths. Weak to Intelligence.
- **Glimmer Moth Swarm** — moths once drawn to torchlight, now magic-warped and sensitive to spellcraft. Weak to Intelligence, resists melee.

**Tier 2 — Deepening Corruption**
- **Hollow Sentinel** — an animated guardian construct, movement gone wrong/jerky. Weak to Intelligence, resists Strength.
- **Rift-touched Stalker** — a creature fused with something not of this world; fast, unpredictable. Weak to Finesse.
- **Rune-Warped Golem** — a guardian construct whose control runes are unstable; break the rune matrix with magic and it collapses. Weak to Intelligence, resists Strength and Finesse.

**Tier 3 — True Demons**
- **Ashbound Wraith** — a genuine demon carrying the ash/ember of Ashwren's fallen homeworld. Weak to Intelligence, resists physical entirely.
- **Rift Hound** — fast demon scout-type, hunts in pairs. Weak to Finesse.
- **Veil Wraith** — barely physical, more shadow than substance; only real magic can touch it. Weak to Intelligence, resists Strength and Finesse.

## The Skeleton Line — Parallel, Uncorrupted Thread (locked in)
Skeletons appear across ALL tiers, deliberately staying uncorrupted throughout — a contrast to the demonic escalation elsewhere. They're a "known quantity" enemy: players can always trust that a skeleton is just bones and force, nothing magical or unpredictable.

**Shared trait:** all weak to Strength, all resist Finesse (no vital points to precisely target — just bone), neutral to Intelligence.

- **Skeleton Warrior** *(Tier 0)* — sword and shield, basic reanimated guardian.
- **Skeleton Archer** *(appears from Tier 1 onward)* — ranged, picks at the party from distance.
- **Skeleton Knight** *(appears from Tier 2 onward)* — heavier armor, more HP, hits hard.
- **Skeleton Juggernaut** *(boss-tier — final boss of the skeleton line)* — a massive, reinforced skeletal construct, bones fused/bolted together with old iron. No corruption, no magic — just overwhelming physical mass. Weak to Strength, but needs sustained damage — a real endurance fight.

## The Mad One — Late-Game Reveal Encounter (locked in)
A devastating story beat: late in the game (before the final confrontation), the party encounters an unidentified enemy — shown in combat only as something like **"???" or "Starved Figure,"** never named. It's feral, half-starved, and driven mad by isolation — but fights with flashes of real trained combat skill (echoes of the weapon-type system), hinting it was once a real fighter, without confirming who. It must be killed to progress.

**Only after the kill** does the truth land: dropped gear reveals the identity — most powerfully, **a weapon with a name scratched into it**, one the player recognizes (a gut-punch tie-in to the weapon-naming mechanic — it's someone's *named* weapon). Alongside it: a broken torch, and possibly a final journal entry showing their decline into starvation and madness.

**Why this works:** the protagonist's whole drive is Hope — refusing to believe their friend might be dead. This forces them to kill someone without knowing, only to learn afterward it was one of the very people they came to find. It should land right before the player starts truly fearing what they'll find with the leader.

## Final Boss: Ashwren Vale (locked in)
Unlike every other enemy, **Ashwren has no weapon-type weakness** — hits always show neutral (blue) particles. This makes him feel genuinely different from anything else in the game: not a creature with a biological weak point, but a grieving, powerful scholar. The fight is instead structured as **3 phases**, tracking his emotional unraveling as HP drops:

- **Phase 1 — The Reasoning Scholar** *(100–66% HP):* He doesn't want to fight. Warns the party away, fights defensively with controlled sealing/warding magic turned reluctantly offensive — slow, methodical.
- **Phase 2 — The Desperate Bargain** *(66–33% HP):* Composure slipping. Draws on rawer, less controlled power — the same unstable magic that failed to save his world. Faster, less predictable attacks; the portal starts bleeding energy into the room as environmental hazards.
- **Phase 3 — The Broken Vow** *(33–0% HP):* Full desperation. The portal cracks further open mid-fight — brief, threatening echoes of whatever destroyed his homeworld flicker through as attack patterns. He's not really fighting the party anymore so much as fighting his own unraveling.

## Spell / Rune System (locked in)
Base rune set — thematically tied to sealing/portal magic, echoing Ashwren's own magical tradition:
- **Ward** — protection/shielding
- **Seal** — binding/restraining (echoes the gem-lock puzzle theme — same magical language)
- **Void** — drain, dark-touched damage
- **Light** — illumination, purifying, strong against dark/demonic things
- **Ember** — fire/ash damage
- **Sight** — perception/insight (e.g. reveals precise enemy HP numbers, complementing the floating HP bar)
- **Rift** — small-scale spatial magic (movement/teleport) — a controlled, responsible echo of the exact magic Ashwren is recklessly attempting at world-scale

**Combo tiers (locked in):**
- **Basic spells:** 4-rune sequences
- **Stronger spells:** 5-rune sequences
- **The most powerful spell in the game:** all 7 runes, each used exactly once, in a specific correct order — a true mastery spell. Thematically strong candidate for the spell that finally seals the rift at the story's climax. *(Name TBD.)*

**Example spells — Basic Tier (all 4-rune combos; exact sequences to be finalized during build):**

*Utility*
- Torch — Light, Light, Ward, Sight
- Unbind Door — Seal, Seal, Rift, Light
- Trailmark — Sight, Rift, Rift, Light — leaves/reveals magical footprints
- Detect Magic — Sight, Light, Sight, Ward
- Identify — Sight, Sight, Light, Seal
- Featherstep — Rift, Ward, Sight, Light — quieter movement, reduces trap-trigger chance
- Mend — Seal, Seal, Ward, Light — repairs a damaged weapon/armor
- Sustain — Ward, Light, Ward, Sight — conjures food/water

*Offense*
- Firebolt — Ember, Ember, Void, Light
- Void Bolt — Void, Void, Ember, Seal
- Weaken Wraith — Light, Void, Sight, Seal — strips defenses from ghostly/wraith-type enemies (direct DM callback; counters Veil Wraith / Ashbound Wraith specifically)
- Draining Cloud — Void, Void, Ember, Ward — lingering damage cloud, careless casters can be hit too
- Spark Bolt — Ember, Sight, Void, Ember — cheaper, lower-damage bolt
- Rift Spike — Rift, Void, Ember, Seal — extra damage vs demonic/corrupted enemies
- Binding Strike — Seal, Seal, Void, Ember — roots the target briefly on hit
- Radiant Burst — Light, Light, Ember, Void — holy fire, strong vs Tier 2/3 demons
- Void Leech — Void, Void, Void, Light — damage + caster recovers a little HP/mana

*Defense*
- Ward Shield — Ward, Ward, Light, Seal
- Fire Ward — Ward, Ember, Ward, Light
- Reflect Ward — Ward, Seal, Ward, Light — chance to reflect some damage back
- Line Ward — Ward, Ward, Seal, Light — shields the front line only

*Perception* (NOTE: perception spells never reveal the minimap — map scrolls are the only way to fill it in; these only reveal things in the current room/immediate area)
- See HP — Sight, Sight, Ward, Light
- True Sight — Sight, Light, Sight, Rift — reveals hidden/invisible things nearby
- Sense Danger — Sight, Seal, Sight, Ward — warns of nearby traps/puzzle mechanisms

*Movement*
- Blink — Rift, Rift, Ward, Light — short-range teleport a few paces
- Rift Step — Rift, Rift, Rift, Light — longer teleport, same room only
- Featherfall — Rift, Ward, Light, Sight — safe landing from height/pit traps

*Healing / Cleanse*
- Heal Wounds — Light, Ward, Light, Ward — single-target minor heal
- Group Mend — Light, Ward, Ward, Light — small heal to the whole party at once
- Regrowth — Light, Light, Ward, Sight — heal-over-time effect
- Cure Affliction — Light, Void, Ward, Seal — cures poison/curse status effects

*Debuff*
- Weaken Foe — Void, Seal, Void, Ward — reduces enemy damage output
- Slow — Seal, Void, Seal, Ward — slows enemy attack/movement speed
- Blind — Light, Seal, Void, Ward — reduces enemy accuracy briefly
- Root — Seal, Seal, Seal, Ward — stronger immobilize than Weaken Foe

**5-Rune (Stronger) Tier — parked for later, purposefully:**
Rather than design all of these now, they'll be built once specific bosses/puzzles exist, so each one has a real reason to be exactly that powerful. Confirmed so far:
- **Super Heal** — large single-target or party-wide HP restore
- **Resurrection** — fully revives a fallen party member
- **Boss-countering spells** — designed around specific boss mechanics once bosses are finalized (e.g. something for Ashwren's Phase 3, or the Skeleton Juggernaut)
- **Puzzle-solving spells** — tied to specific environmental puzzles. First confirmed example: **Super Light** — an intensely powerful light spell specifically required to see/navigate a dark, wet section of the dungeon
- **Rift Walk** — teleport to any already-discovered square on the map — Rift, Rift, Sight, Sight, Ward

**Discovery methods (locked in — both apply):**
- **Taught** — a scroll spells out the exact rune combo directly
- **Guessed** — the player experiments with rune sequences and stumbles onto a working combo, unlocking it permanently
- **Hidden spells** — rarer, more powerful combos never hinted by any scroll — pure experimentation, or found only through sources like Ashwren's own research notes or the rival leader's personal spellbook
